Purchase-to-pay process insights

Most invoices that take longer than 60 days to pay are probably not a process failure: 68% of them sit in one spend area (Packaging), which pays at a median of 69 days — the signature of longer agreed terms. The delays the company does control are upstream: invoices wait a median 9.6 days to be booked, and goods receipts are posted late enough to hold payments up.

Weekly manual workload

Steps done by people, with the week-of-month forecast, backtested 4 weeks ahead.

  1. Check the terms before chasing “late” payments. 42% of PO lines are paid after 60 days, but 57 vendors (3% of them) account for 80% of those lines, and Packaging alone accounts for 68%. Action: Pull the contract terms for the 57 vendors. Where terms are 60 days or less, this is real late payment; where they are longer, re-baseline the KPI so it stops flagging agreed behaviour.
  2. Invoices wait to be booked. Median 9.6 days from the vendor's invoice date to booking; 42% wait over two weeks. Booking is 96% manual, done by 29 people (219,288 bookings), although batch jobs already book 9,472. Action: Pilot e-invoicing / OCR capture with the highest-volume vendors and track invoice-date-to-booking weekly, with a target median of 3 days.
  3. Late goods receipts hold payments. When the invoice arrives before the goods receipt is posted, the line is paid after 60 days 7.7 percentage points more often — comparing within the same vendor (589 vendors). Blocked invoices spend 33% of their wait before the block is lifted (median 6.0 days). Action: Set a 2-working-day posting target for goods receipts and report it by site. GR posting is 73% manual across 246 people; batch already posts 85,034, so extend automatic receipt to the vendors that support it.
  4. POs raised after the invoice. 3,621 lines (1.4%, €49.4M) were ordered after the vendor had already invoiced — a median 12 days later. Trading & End Products produces 40% of them; in Real Estate, 52% of lines are retroactive. Action: Enforce “no PO, no pay” in Trading & End Products and Real Estate first, and brief the buyers behind the 83 vendors that make up 80% of cases.
  5. Staff for week 4 of the month, not the average week. Manual work in the fourth week of the month (days 22-28) runs 25% above the other weeks. A forecast that knows this misses by 11% four weeks ahead, against 17% for a flat 4-week average (6 backtest folds). The obvious suspect, month-end close, does not explain it: weeks holding a month end are only 6% busier. Action: Plan the accounts-payable and receiving rota from the week-of-month forecast. Find out what lands in week 4 (a payment-run or reporting cut-off is the likely candidate) before moving work out of it.
